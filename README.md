Faxbot is a proprietary, self-hosted fax platform with a web Admin Console, a REST API, cloud and SIP providers, and integrations for AI assistants. It helps organizations send and receive documents, choose delivery routes, track costs, and deliver incoming documents to the inboxes staff already use.

This README describes the current source checkout. Published packages and deployed installations may have fewer features; the [roadmap](#roadmap) distinguishes implemented capabilities from planned work and experiments.

## What Faxbot does

- **Send and receive faxes.** Prepare PDF, TXT, and supported TIFF documents, track outbound jobs, and organize received faxes in mailboxes. Uncertain submissions wait for confirmation instead of being blindly sent again. A received fax shows as waiting until Faxbot has fetched and checked its real document; see [receiving faxes](docs/operations/receiving.md).
- **Choose providers independently.** Use separate outbound and inbound providers. Built-in adapters cover Phaxio, Sinch, Documo, HumbleFax, eFax, SignalWire and SIP/Asterisk; supported operations vary by provider. Additional HTTP providers can use manifests. See [provider setup](docs/setup/index.md).
- **Fax through your own SIP carrier or phone system.** Connect a carrier SIP trunk (Telnyx first; SignalWire, Sinch, AnveoDirect, Flowroute, Gamma and BT One Voice in the UK, Telstra SIP Connect in Australia, or another) to the built-in Asterisk engine, or fax through your office's Avaya IP Office or Aura phone system on the local network. Fax over T.38 by the minute with no per-page fee, and keep a record of every call. See [SIP trunk setup](docs/setup/sip-trunk.md) and [Avaya IP Office and Aura](docs/setup/avaya.md).
- **Choose delivery routes and track spending.** Configure additional outbound routes, rate cards (including flat monthly plans), and destination preferences. Faxbot uses price estimates and delivery history to rank routes, records each attempt, and reconciles reported SignalWire charges and, with a `TELNYX_API_KEY`, what Telnyx billed for each sent and received trunk call, separately from delivery status. Unknown charges stay unknown and a route with an unknown cost is never called the cheapest. A sent fax or received call with no charge and no estimate (for example an answered call whose length was never reported) is left out of every total and counted as "not priced yet" beside it, never as $0. See [delivery routes](docs/operations/delivery-routes.md).
- **Use the price for the number actually called.** Recipient recommendations and attempt estimates use the account's applicable destination tariff, including approved alternate numbers and prices entered for where calls start. A domestic rate does not stand in for a missing international rate. A monthly allowance applies only where its tariff covers the call; a separately priced international fax is not labeled included.
- **Compare setup plans.** In **Savings & optimization → Facts to establish**, compare up to ten setup items under your stated currency, spending limit and time period. Enter shared costs and expected and cautious running-cost reductions; Faxbot finds the best affordable group or recommends keeping what is installed. The inputs are not saved and the plan changes no routes or partner enrollments.
- **Deliver incoming documents by email.** One intake queue collects ordinary faxes and direct deliveries. SMTP connectors send the received PDF to configured inboxes, with retries for confirmed temporary failures and review for uncertain outcomes. See [intake](docs/operations/intake.md).
- **Hand received documents to an owner.** **Faxes → Received** and `faxbot faxes received owners` give each received document an owner who acknowledges it and marks it done, with an optional operational acknowledgement target per installation or mailbox, one escalation to a backup person, and a per-item evidence export. An owner must already be able to see the document; assignment never grants access. See [Owners in Received](docs/operations/work.md).
- **Send directly to verified Faxbot partners.** Enrolled installations can exchange encrypted original PDFs, or the exact fax image unless the receiving partner turns that off, and signed receipts using the recipient's usual fax number, with no telephone call. Every direct arrival is filed as a received fax with an owner. Enrollment requires both installations and a fax challenge; ordinary fax fallback preserves the delivery's identity and uncertainty checks. See [direct delivery](docs/operations/direct-delivery.md).
- **Send short faxes to one number together.** When a recipient agrees, faxes to its number can wait a few minutes and share one SIP trunk call, which saves per-call minimums. See [sending together](docs/operations/delivery-routes.md#sending-together).
- **Notice the fax that never came.** **Faxes → Expected** and `faxbot faxes expected` record a fax before it arrives: its reference (such as PO 483), what should come, from whom and by when. Faxbot closes it only when a received fax carries that exact reference in its subaddress, a partner's registered form, a Direct message it answers or an email subject pattern you set; a fax from a known number, partner or address is only proposed for you to confirm. Open work can be imported from another system's CSV or JSON export, and after that system's outage the next export is sorted into already done, new and held for you. See [Expected faxes](#proposed-enterprise-foundation).
- **Avoid repeating acknowledged case documents.** A case packet lists the documents a recipient has acknowledged on a one-page index and sends only new or changed ones, when an administrator records that the recipient accepts references. A delivered fax alone does not count as acknowledgement. The recipient acknowledges a document through a partner's signed receipt, the receiving team acknowledging a packet delivered to one of your own numbers, or a person recording what the recipient confirmed. Each acknowledgement is trusted for the recipient's reuse period, which is 90 days unless you set another. If the recipient cannot find a document, the next packet sends it in full, and a person can resend the whole packet as a new fax. Faxbot checks the recipient's reference setting; collecting evidence of that consent is future work. Preview packets before sending. See [case packets](docs/operations/delivery-routes.md#case-packets).
- **Manage people and integrations.** Named users, groups, roles, mailbox permissions, scoped API keys, sessions, and mobile pairing are managed in the console. The server enforces the same permissions. See [access control](docs/security/access-control.md).

## Admin Console

The API serves the console at `/admin/ui/`. Its left panel has six areas, each page at its own address (such as `#/delivery/trunk`), and **Send a fax** at the top for people who may send:

- **Overview**: what Faxbot is doing for you (the capabilities that acted on your faxes and the results, each in its own unit), the next improvements, everyday faxes with Send a fax, and what needs a person now, grouped by the action it needs and each opening its exact list; a serious problem moves to the top while it lasts. At the bottom, a map of every way Faxbot saves money, along a fax's path, with whether each is on, works here and has been tested.
- **Savings & optimization**: capabilities, opportunities, facts to establish, savings results (estimates), spending, charges, invoices, and prices and plans.
- **Faxes**: Received (with each fax's owner), Sent (route, cost, delivery attempts), Send a fax (route and cost before sending), Expected, Forms and Case packets.
- **Delivery setup**: under Numbers & mailboxes, every number Faxbot carries, mailboxes, number moves, blocked senders and sending identity; under Documents in and out, staff email delivery and the email and folders that bring documents in; under Connections, the providers and accounts in use, routing rules, one page per provider in use (the trunk named by its carrier), and **Add or change a provider**, which opens the Setup wizard.
- **Recipients**: the numbers you fax and their route order, and direct-delivery partners.
- **Administration**: under People & access, users, groups, roles, who has access, keys and phones, and sessions; under Installation, setup, security, documents and retention, AI analysis and the NPI record; under Monitoring, system health, the audit log and logs; and Developer (API and SDKs, AI assistants, terminal, scripts and checks, provider plugins).

Every address from before the six areas still opens the page that does that job now, with its query, and says once where the page moved from. An address that names no page, or a page you may not open, shows its own short message instead.

Screens follow the signed-in user's permissions, use plain words outside Developer, and show settings only the owner may change disabled to everyone else. See the [console guide](docs/admin-console.md) and [Setup Wizard](docs/admin-console/setup-wizard.md).

## Quick start with Docker Compose

1. Copy the bootstrap configuration:

   ```sh
   cp .env.example .env
   ```

2. Replace the example credentials, set a strong installation `API_KEY`, and choose a provider using its [setup guide](docs/setup/index.md). For an initial document-only evaluation, set `FAX_DISABLED=true` before the first startup: jobs remain held and are not reported as successful transmissions.
3. Set the public URL and console origins for your deployment. Console sessions require HTTPS. For an HTTP-only Docker evaluation on a trusted private network, explicitly set `FAXBOT_ALLOW_INSECURE_HTTP_SESSIONS=true`; see [authentication](docs/security/authentication.md#https-and-plain-http) for the transport requirements.
4. Build and start the API and console:

   ```sh
   docker compose up -d --build api
   ```

   SIP/Asterisk installations also need the `asterisk` service and a configured trunk; follow the [Asterisk guide](docs/setup/sip-asterisk.md).

5. Open `http://localhost:8080/admin/ui/` for a local HTTP evaluation, or `/admin/ui/` on your configured HTTPS address. Sign in with the installation key to [create the first owner](docs/security/access-control.md#create-the-first-owner), then use a named account for daily work.

Health checks are available at `/health` and `/health/ready`. `/health/ready` answers 200 when Faxbot is ready for what it is set up to do (sending, receiving or both) and 503 otherwise, the same rule `faxbot admin health` uses for its exit code. Readiness does not prove that a fax has been delivered. Faxbot starts even when it cannot sign in to its fax engine (Asterisk): readiness, the dashboard and Settings give the reason in one sentence, sends are refused with it, and Faxbot keeps trying. The Compose file restarts the `api` and `asterisk` services after any exit, including **Restart Faxbot** in the console.

Credentials in `.env` (provider keys, passwords and secrets) are read at every start and are the values in force; the console shows them as **Set in .env** and they are changed there, followed by `docker compose up -d` (a plain `docker compose restart` keeps the old values). `API_KEY` (the installation key) is read only at the first start, and other settings are managed in the console after that. Follow any requested restart (Settings offers **Restart now**) and confirm the active settings before transmitting. [Held test jobs](docs/setup/test-mode.md) remain held when sending is enabled later.

## AI analysis

Open **Administration → AI analysis** to choose OpenAI, OpenRouter, or another OpenAI-compatible API. Enter the API base address, model identifier and API key, save them, and test the connection. The owner can then enable analysis and choose a refresh interval; zero means manual refresh only. Keys are encrypted with the installation's existing secret storage and are never returned in full to the console or command line.

**Run analysis** queues a saved report. The model can read bounded operational evidence about outbound delivery outcomes, attempt costs and calls avoided. It receives no fax documents, fax numbers or saved provider credentials. It cannot send a fax or change settings. Existing cost calculations and rule-based recommendations continue to update from records without an LLM. AI advice is additional interpretation; estimates, reported charges and settlements remain separate, and the analysis does not claim to total subscriptions, inbound charges or unmatched bills.

The console shows when analysis last completed, its model and evidence, whether it is stale, and any refresh failure. A failed refresh keeps the previous successful report. The same controls are available with `faxbot admin analysis configure`, `test`, `run` and `status`; `faxbot savings analysis` reads the saved report. Use `--key-prompt` or `--key-stdin` when configuring the model key. No model provider is contacted until you enable analysis or explicitly test its connection.

## SDKs and AI assistants

Use the [Python SDK](docs/sdks/python.md) or [Node.js SDK](docs/sdks/node.md) to call Faxbot's API:

```sh
pip install faxbot
npm install faxbot
```

Another system can hand a PDF to the work queue with `POST /imports` and a small JSON manifest; see [import documents](docs/operations/work.md#import-documents-from-another-system). An email mailbox or a watched folder can do the same without any code, under **Delivery setup → Email and folders** (`faxbot delivery connectors`).

Faxbot's AI assistant servers (Node and Python) work over stdio and Streamable HTTP. The Python server also offers the older SSE transport, which is removed in the next release. Remote requests use the caller's own Faxbot identity. See [MCP setup](docs/mcp/index.md) and [transport authentication](docs/mcp/transports.md).

## Command line

The `faxbot` command does what the Admin Console does, apart from its built-in terminal, under the same six areas: after `faxbot send` and `faxbot status` come `overview`, `savings`, `faxes`, `delivery`, `recipients` and `admin`. The command names from before the six areas (`received`, `sent`, `forms`, `expected`, `numbers`, `providers`, `costs`, `access` and `system`, and renamed commands such as `costs savings`) still work, hidden from help, so scripts keep running. Every setting can be read and changed with `faxbot admin settings`. Output is readable by default and `--json` for scripts.

```
docker compose exec api faxbot admin health
FAXBOT_API_KEY=... faxbot send +15551234567 referral.pdf
```

With Faxbot stopped, `faxbot admin recover-owner`, `backup`, `restore` and `migrate` recover owner access, back up and restore an installation, and upgrade its database. See [Command line](docs/operations/cli.md) and the [command reference](docs/reference/cli.md).

A number holds faxes only on a trunk billed per call or with a minimum. - [x] The fax coding measured on each fax's own pages, and calls priced by their expected bill. Before each attempt on Faxbot's own engines, the layout chooser measures the lossless codings MH, MR, MMR and JBIG on that attempt's pages (JBIG with jbigkit's T.85 tools, installed in the API image, and with the options the SSL Fax engine sends with, so the measured size is the size on the line) and prices each layout with the smallest coding the call may use. MMR and JBIG are used only with error correction, which Faxbot never turns off for them. A coding that failed twice in a row to the number is never used, nor anything past your compression setting. On AR's synthetic pages, MH came out 20% smaller than MMR on a shaded table, 30% on a tinted form and 34% on a noisy gray scan, while MMR was about half the size of MH on black text; the numbers match Codex's libtiff 4.7.1 measurements exactly. The shared predictor uses the measured sizes and labels its old fixed ratios as an estimate. It now prices a call by its expected bill over the spread of its time rather than by rounding the average: a call of 59 or 61 seconds with equal odds bills 90 seconds expected on 60-second steps. Each number's spread is learned from its own recorded calls, with an assumed 15% either way until it has 3. The built-in engine has no JBIG and gets the smallest measured coding. Faxbot now hands it back through the engine's job controls, and engine learning's compression change now goes the same way (not yet proven on a call of its own). On the built-in engine it goes as FAXBOT_COMPRESSION, which Asterisk patch 0006 reads. - [x] Power-aware sending (**Administration → System health → Power**, `faxbot admin diagnostics power show|set|off`, `GET`/`PUT /power`, migration 0072). It then waits for you to check it again in Sent; it is not yet released by itself when the power returns. Tested with a fake NUT server; not yet run against a real UPS.

- [x] NTT Hikari Denwa in Japan, built from what NTT publishes (**Delivery setup → carrier trunk → NTT Hikari Denwa**, `SIP_TRUNK_PRESET=ntt-hikari`). The trunk signs in over IPv4 by UDP or TCP to the SIP server NTT's network hands out by DHCP, sends G.711 mu-law and dials national 0AB-J numbers. NTT's interface lists the Japanese T.38 standard and allows fax over IP between Data Connect devices, but its detailed interface is not published, so Faxbot offers T.38 exactly as ITU-T T.38 Annex D describes it and says to confirm the settings with NTT first. The rate card prices an ordinary call at NTT East's ¥8.8 per 3 minutes to fixed lines, and Data Connect is priced by the bandwidth held (¥1.1, ¥1.65 or ¥2.2 per 30 seconds up to 64 kbit/s, 512 kbit/s or 1 Mbit/s, and ¥16.5 or ¥110 per 3 minutes for several sessions at once, tax included, read 10 October 2026 from NTT East's price page and NTT West's Data Connect manual), counting every started unit. The trunk page states NTT's documented limits: Data Connect only between Data Connect devices on FLET'S Hikari Next, charged while the bandwidth is held, FAX notification email not working for Data Connect faxes (and ending in March 2028), and residential Hikari Denwa carrying fax as voice. Not yet run against NTT.

### Proposed enterprise foundation

Future work will extend Faxbot from document delivery into accountable correspondence across industries. Build on existing access controls and delivery infrastructure, retaining separate inbound and outbound providers. The [enterprise architecture](planning/enterprise-correspondence.md) maps implemented foundations, partial capabilities and proposed interfaces. This direction does not expand Phase 1 or the four fixes above.

- [x] **E0 — Trustworthy acquisition:** a received fax is recorded only from a checked notification and waits until its real document has arrived. It resumes after a restart or a repeated notification, and keeps its provider fax ID, account, receipt time and document digest. See [receiving faxes](docs/operations/receiving.md).
- [x] **E1 — Smallest useful workflow:** one generic import (`POST /imports`, `faxbot faxes received import`), a mailbox-scoped owned queue (owners in **Faxes → Received**, `faxbot faxes received owners`), operational acknowledgement targets with one escalation to a backup person, and a permission-scoped evidence export. Transport delivery, email delivery and acknowledgement remain separate events. Tested with synthetic documents only; not validated against a customer system. See [Owners in Received](docs/operations/work.md).
- [x] **Expected faxes (research CE3 and CE6), on E1's work model:** **Faxes → Expected** and `faxbot faxes expected` hold an expectation (reference, what is expected, from whom, owning mailbox, due time as an operational target) before anything arrives. Only a stable reference closes one: the exact reference in the fax subaddress, a partner's registered form field, a Direct message ID or the message it answers (In-Reply-To, References), or an email subject pattern the admin saves. A known sender's number, partner or Direct address within the window, or text from an approved extraction, only proposes a match for a person. Required parts never close automatically, and a required revision closes only when the arrival states that revision. Overdue expectations escalate once to the mailbox's backup person. A saved CSV or JSON column mapping imports open work under the generic import's identity rules: replays add nothing, a changed row waits for review, a new revision replaces an open one, and rows a later full export no longer lists are reported and stay open. Outage mode records what was done by fax or email while a source system was down, against original IDs, and sorts the next export into already done, new and held lists; Faxbot never sends or submits anything for it. The report lists missing expectations and received faxes that answered nothing apart from the match rate, and each expectation has an evidence export. Matching runs when a received fax becomes work, so an idle installation does no matching. Migration 0064. Tested with synthetic data on SQLite and PostgreSQL; not run against a real ERP, EHR or partner yet, and no OCR or extraction service exists to call the proposal-only extraction entry point.
- [ ] **E2 — Protected workflows:** a shared versioned policy foundation, exact-action approvals, verified recipient/purpose/channel policies, configurable retention and legal holds across managed copies.
- [ ] **E3 — Templates and guided setup:** optional, source-backed jurisdiction/industry templates; organization, mailbox and workflow settings; explained suggestions and visible missing requirements. Prioritize UK, Australian and US healthcare and other regulated workflows without shipping unverified legal defaults. Guided setup's preview, apply, explained suggestions, missing list and per-mailbox settings are built (Suggested Packs, above); the source-backed templates and workflow-level settings are not.
- [ ] **E4 — Enterprise integrations:** workforce identity and provisioning, controlled OCR/AI processing and case-system connectors that preserve evidence and reconcile uncertain outcomes.

Any control required by a selected real workflow is a prerequisite to using its records, regardless of stage number. Templates must state their scope, version, required settings, evidence and remaining organizational responsibilities. Customer-specific forms and integrations stay optional; these capabilities are not yet implemented as an enterprise workflow.

Enterprise software acceptance uses synthetic/local tests. Live customer end-to-end validation is separate and never a CI, merge or release requirement; see the [testing boundary](CONTRIBUTING.md#enterprise-testing-boundary).

- [x] Resend only the missing pieces of a document to an enrolled partner after a broken transfer, with the document assembled and committed once on the receiving side. A document over 4 MiB goes in 1 MiB pieces after a signed preflight, in which the receiver can refuse early with its reason (sender, recipient, type, size, page count, freshness, room on disk) before any document byte is sent. Each piece is kept once, and only when its SHA-256 is the signed one. After a dropped connection, the receiver lists the missing pieces by sequence and SHA-256 and only those go again. The commit checks every hash and records the document once, with the same signed receipt as a single delivery. Unfinished transfers are resumed when the partner answers again, and stopped and fenced after 23 hours. **Recipients → Partners → Documents sent in pieces**, `faxbot recipients partners transfers`. Tested with two synthetic installations.
- [x] Partners on your own network with their own certificate: once partner discovery (migration 0045, merging separately) has pinned a partner's certificate, Faxbot accepts exactly that certificate for its address and verifies every other address normally, never without verification. A changed certificate is refused before anything is sent, and the partner shows "Its certificate is not the one it had when you enrolled it". Tested against a local TLS server.
- [x] Send once to an enrolled partner whose intake files faxes for several of its numbers, only where that partner agrees. The receiving organization offers it, signed, naming the exact numbers its intake files for; the sender's administrator accepts it, because faxes to those numbers then stop going by telephone; either side ends it at once (**Recipients → Partners → Send once**, `faxbot recipients partners send-once list|offer|accept|end`). Similar numbers or a shared name never count. Each fax to a covered number still goes as its own direct delivery with its own signed receipt and Sent row. When the first of several faxes from one sender, with the same document to different covered numbers, goes out, the others of that send already waiting (accepted within ten minutes of it) are named with it: it carries the document and a signed list of those recipients, they send only a reference to that copy, and each one's Sent row says "Sent once to {partner}'s intake, which delivers to {n} recipients." A fax of the same document accepted after the first one went is not on that list; it still goes as a reference to the copy the partner holds, and says "Sent to {partner}'s intake, which files it for this number." Faxbot never holds a fax back to wait for others, and never groups two faxes to the same number. The intake files each recipient's copy, by its own receiving rules, only when that recipient's own delivery arrives, so a fax that went by telephone instead is never filed twice. Each copy is its own received fax and Work item, and a number no rule places waits in Received with the reason. Its receipt lists where each recipient is filed. Migration 0049. Tested with two synthetic installations; no real partner intake has used it.
- [x] Send an enrolled partner only the bytes it lacks, for original documents and fax images. Before an original goes to a partner that accepted the exact same document (SHA-256) from you before, Faxbot asks, signed, whether it still holds it, and then sends only a reference that the partner files from its kept copy. For a newer version of a case document (same case, source and purpose in the case ledger), it sends only the changes from the version the partner holds, as a binary delta. The partner files the result only when its SHA-256 matches the signed manifest. A partner answers only about documents that partner delivered to it. On any miss the whole document goes at once. These save bytes, never money, because the call was already avoided: **Savings & optimization → Savings results → Bytes saved by reuse and patches** and `faxbot savings results` show them apart from money. A fax image differs on every send only in the header line Faxbot prints above each page (the date, time and page number), so Faxbot writes each page's body apart from that header band and names the body by its digest; the partner's signed receipt for a fax image says which body it now holds. When a partner signed for the same body to the same number and says, signed, that it still holds it, only the new header lines and the image's structure go, and the partner puts its kept body back and files the image only when its SHA-256 matches the signed manifest. A missing copy, an image that does not match, an answer more than 10 minutes old or another recipient sends the whole image at once, and Sent says "only the new header lines went" when they were all that went. Keeping each page's body apart makes every fax image about 3% larger on a dense page and up to 11% on a light one. Tested with two synthetic installations on SQLite and PostgreSQL; not yet run between two live installations.

- [x] Encoded pages (experimental): a whole document can travel as a few fax pages and be recovered byte for byte. It is off by default and requires the recipient's agreement (**Recipients → Details → Encoded pages**, `faxbot recipients encoded`). With comparable prices, the route predictor selects encoded pages only for a lower estimated bill. On a plan or an unpriced route, it can select fewer pages while rejecting a known increase in transmission time; that is not a claim of monetary savings. The same chooser compares ordinary, packed and encoded pages for each attempt. On Faxbot's own engines, it now measures every encoded candidate in the usable fax codings before rejecting or ranking it; an MMR-only estimate can no longer hide a candidate that costs less in MH. Each engine is then asked for that coding: on a loopback call (10 October 2026) the SSL Fax engine sent a payload page in JBIG, as measured, and the built-in engine, which has no JBIG, in MR, the smallest of the others, where it had previously taken MMR by itself. Where a provider draws the pages, every coding is still measured on them and the estimate uses the measured size of the coding the call is expected to use, not a fixed ratio to MMR, which overpriced payload pages. Encoded pages are never packed, trimmed or lightened, and the original files remain unchanged.

  Grid pages tolerate resolution changes; run-coded pages need an exact raster; picture pages carry the document in their dot pattern. These three layouts also work with the static decoder in `tools/fax-decoder`. A fourth layout, **enumerative profile 1**, maps payload bits into bounded-cost MH patterns. It is available explicitly through `faxbot admin codec encode original.pdf --layout enumerative --output payload.tiff`; use `faxbot admin codec decode received.pdf --output original.pdf` with a Faxbot version that supports this profile. It is not selected automatically and the static browser decoder does not support it. Its page carries the profile and recovery parameters. Unknown profiles, invalid bounds and contradictory payload rows are refused. A fifth layout, **capacity** (payload format 2), draws the payload as runs at the capacity of the complete T.4 code with an arithmetic coder over frozen frequency tables, in three profiles: *time* for the shortest call, *pages* for the fewest pages, and *balanced*. Measured on full fine pages of random payload, in MH at 14,400 bit/s: *time* carries a kilobyte in 0.663 seconds, 4% less than the best run-coded limit for time (63: 0.689) and 7% less than the default (15: 0.716); *pages* carries 357 kB a page, 14% more than the best run-coded limit for pages (7: 313 kB) and 55% more than the default (231 kB); in JBIG every profile is faster still. These are well below the research bound (17–19% and two fifths), which compared ideal sources with the default limit only and left out each line's offset, CRC and padding and each page's caption and ladder. Faxbot chooses it by itself only for a recipient whose decoder you recorded as reading it (**Recipients → Details → Encoded pages → Recipient's decoder**, `faxbot recipients encoded set NUMBER --decoder capacity`, database revision 0071): the chooser then also prices the *time* and *pages* profiles and the route's bill decides; every other recipient keeps the format 1 layouts. `faxbot admin codec encode --layout capacity --capacity-profile time|balanced|pages` makes the pages explicitly. A Faxbot decoder from October 2026 or later and the browser decoder in `tools/fax-decoder` read them; a page of a newer format, layout or profile is refused in one sentence. A partner Faxbot's signed capabilities do not yet say which layouts its decoder reads: this release reads capability statements that carry keys it does not know, so that key can follow in the next. Decoding reads what real receivers publish: a public test line's PDF receipt (one CCITT image per page, one white row short), page pictures without a resolution, CCITT Group 3 one- and two-dimensional and Group 4, Flate and image-mask PDFs, pages stored in strips, pages turned upside down or inverted, a header line drawn over the top or added above, square pixels, and run-coded rows moved sideways or padded to letter width. A page drawn again at another size, which run-coded and enumerative pages cannot survive, or a small preview instead of the fax, is refused in one sentence that says what to decode instead. Proven with synthetic fixtures of each container and change for every layout; no encoded page has yet been decoded from a real third-party receipt.

  All layouts retain the original SHA-256 check and Reed–Solomon correction; an agreed shared key encrypts the document with AES-256-GCM. A receiving Faxbot keeps the received image and either recovers the original or reports why it could not. After you add, change or clear a stored shared key, opening Received details or downloading the decoded original retries a failed recovery; an unchanged key does not repeatedly retry the same failed document. Verified originals stay local even when received fax images use object storage, and follow the received fax’s retention deadline. Expiration removes both copies and preserves the decode history, with the download unavailable. If checking an encoded original fails unexpectedly during email delivery, the email says so and still includes the fax as received. Sent and Received details, `faxbot faxes sent show`, `faxbot faxes received show`, and **Savings & optimization → Savings results** retain the attempt and decode evidence. Decoder compatibility, recipient agreement and the applicable document-handling requirements must be established before use; the feature does not establish compliance or freedom to operate.

  On 9 October 2026, an explicitly encoded one-page enumerative fax sent through Telnyx to HumbleFax recovered the exact 55,422-byte synthetic 30-page PDF from the provider's received PDF, with no damaged payload rows. The call used JBIG with error correction at 9,600 bit/s and lasted 69 connected seconds (59 seconds of fax transfer). The existing run-coded representation of the identical document also recovered exactly: 72 connected seconds, 63 seconds of fax transfer, and 420,576 measured JBIG bits versus 397,944 for the enumerative page (5.38% fewer). Telnyx reported $0.01 for each call, so this pair showed no monetary saving. This is one measured carrier path and one comparison pair, not a universal interoperability or timing claim. Synthetic checks cover the supported raster resolutions, MH/MMR recoding, encrypted multipage recovery, damaged and duplicate rows, bounded malformed headers, and compatibility with earlier layouts. In an equal-envelope local comparison, the new mapping used 6.78% fewer canonical MH bits than the existing run mapping; the actual live call used JBIG. Call duration, billed units and money are measured separately.
  Separate LAN calls between ARM64 and x86-64 Asterisk 22.11.0 fax engines also recovered the same original in both directions using T.38 at 14,400 bit/s, with identical received rasters. Two further calls passed the complete Faxbot workflow in fresh installations on those hosts: API submission, durable outbound success, SIP/T.38 transmission, inbound acquisition, automatic decoding, and downloading the exact original through the receiving API. Both calls carried one page and recorded 52 connected seconds, with unchanged queued and received rasters and no codec repairs. These were isolated synthetic installations; existing provider accounts and services remained unchanged.
